Why our mulch is black — and stays black.

Our rubber is cut exclusively from semi-truck tires, not passenger tires. When passenger tires get recycled, their painted and coated whitewalls end up mixed into the rubber stream — that paint is where a lot of "black" rubber mulch actually gets its color. It's also what fades, chalks, and rubs off onto hands, shoes, and clothes over time.

Semi-truck tires don't have whitewalls. There's no paint to begin with — the black is the rubber itself. Our mulch doesn't fade in the sun, doesn't bleed color in the rain, and looks the same five years in as it did the day it was installed. That's why unpainted black is our most popular color.

Every supersack we ship — 2,000 lb, palletized, ready to come off the truck with a forklift or tractor forks — is tested to ASTM F1292 impact-attenuation standards, meeting IPEMA guidance for playground fall-height protection at the depths schools and municipalities are required to install.
